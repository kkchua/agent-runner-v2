# Visual Style Rules (MANDATORY — PHOTOREALISTIC ONLY)

CRITICAL: ALL image prompts (t2i_prompt1) MUST produce photorealistic,
photographic-quality images. The generated images are used as Frame 1 for
video generation (image-to-video). Non-photographic rendering styles
produce poor video results: flickering, morphing, texture instability, and
temporal inconsistency during motion.

Every variant MUST use a real-camera photographic style. Visual variety
between variants must come from different photographic techniques
(lighting, lens, framing, color grading) — never from switching art medium.

## How to Create Visual Variety (Photographic Techniques Only)

Each variant should differ by combining different photographic approaches:

- **Lighting**: natural sunlight, golden hour, blue hour, overcast, studio strobe, side lighting, backlighting, rim lighting
- **Lens/focal length**: wide angle (14mm–35mm), standard (50mm), telephoto (85mm–200mm), macro (100mm+)
- **Depth of field**: deep focus (f/16, everything sharp), shallow depth of field (f/1.4–f/2.8, creamy bokeh)
- **Camera position/height**: ground level, eye level, high angle, bird's eye, crane shot, aerial
- **Color grading**: warm tones, cool tones, desaturated, high contrast, film stock emulation
- **Atmosphere**: clear, overcast, foggy, misty, stormy, hazy
- **Exposure style**: high-key (bright, low contrast), low-key (dark, dramatic shadows), HDR

Choose a DIFFERENT combination of these photographic parameters for each variant.
Do not reuse the same combination. Each variant should feel like it was
photographed by a different professional photographer using different equipment
and techniques.

## Strictly Forbidden Styles (Will Break Video Rendering)

- Oil painting / impasto / brushstroke textures
- Watercolor / ink wash
- Digital painting / concept art / matte painting
- Anime / cartoon / cel-shaded
- Illustration / pen and ink / graphic art
- Sketch / charcoal / pencil
- 3D render / CGI look
- Pixel art / voxel
- Pointillism / stipple
- Any non-photographic art medium

## How to Add "painterly" or "artistic" Quality Without Breaking Photorealism

If the source image description suggests an artistic or stylized feel,
achieve it through PHOTOGRAPHIC means only:
- Soft bokeh and shallow depth of field (not brushstrokes)
- Golden hour or blue hour lighting (not painted skies)
- Film grain and analog color tones (not watercolor washes)
- Lens flare and optical effects (not digital painting overlays)
- Selective focus and tilt-shift (not illustration techniques)

This applies to every variant without exception.

# Variant Generation

Read the `num_variants` field from {MEDIA_CONFIG} to determine how many
variants to generate (default: 4).

Generate exactly N variants where N = num_variants.

Each variant must be a genuinely different photographic interpretation of the
same scene. Visual variety comes from different combinations of camera
position, lighting, lens, depth of field, color grading, and atmosphere —
NOT from changing the subject, environment, or semantic content.

## Guidance for Creating N Diverse Variants

For each variant, independently choose a different combination of:

- Camera position and height (ground level, eye level, high angle, aerial)
- Lens and focal length (wide angle, standard, telephoto, macro)
- Depth of field (deep focus, shallow bokeh)
- Lighting (natural, golden hour, overcast, studio, side-lit, back-lit)
- Color grading (warm, cool, desaturated, high contrast, film emulation)
- Atmosphere (clear, foggy, misty, dramatic)
- Framing (close-up, medium, wide, extreme wide)

Example combinations for 4 variants:
- Variant 1: Natural daylight, eye-level, 50mm lens, deep focus, warm tones
- Variant 2: Golden hour, low angle, 35mm wide angle, shallow bokeh, amber grading
- Variant 3: Overcast, high angle, 85mm telephoto, soft diffused light, desaturated
- Variant 4: Blue hour, aerial, 24mm ultra-wide, long exposure, cool tones

For N > 4, continue exploring additional combinations.
For N > 5, add more dimensions to vary:
- Different times of day (dawn, midday, sunset, blue hour, night)
- Different weather conditions (clear, overcast, rain, snow — if consistent)
- Different perspectives (Dutch angle, worm's eye, bird's eye, hip-level)
- Different photographic styles (documentary, editorial, landscape, macro, wildlife)
- Different film/emulsion looks (Kodak Portra, Fujifilm, Ilford black & white)

All N variants MUST remain photorealistic. No exceptions.

# Video Prompt Rules

Assume the generated image is Frame 1.

Describe only plausible motion.

## Camera Motion — STABLE ONLY (CRITICAL)

The video API sometimes misinterprets camera motion descriptions as handheld
walking, which produces an unpleasant footstep-bounce shake effect.

### ABSOLUTE RULE: Use smooth, stable camera motion only

Camera movement must feel like it was shot on a professional dolly, crane,
or gimbal — NEVER like a person walking holding a camera.

### Safe camera motions (use these):

- **Static camera** — no camera movement, only subject/environment motion
- **Slow dolly in/out** — smooth forward/backward glide (NOT walking)
- **Slow pan** — smooth horizontal rotation
- **Slow tilt** — smooth vertical rotation
- **Slow push-in** — gradual zoom-like approach (not walking forward)
- **Slow pull-back** — gradual retreat (not walking backward)

### Forbidden camera descriptions (will cause shake):

- Walking / walking toward / walking through
- Handheld / shaky / documentary style
- POV / point-of-view / what you see
- Following a subject (implies walking behind them)
- "Camera moves through" (implies walking path)
- Any description that suggests body movement or footstep rhythm
- Fast or jerky movements
- Random camera adjustments

### When in doubt, use "static camera"

A locked-off camera with only subject/environment motion is ALWAYS
preferred over any camera movement that could be misinterpreted as walking.

If you are unsure whether a camera motion will be stable, use "static camera"
or omit camera motion entirely — let the scene move naturally.

### Camera motion examples

CORRECT:
- "Static camera, gentle waves lap at the shore"
- "Slow dolly forward, the subject walks toward the camera"
- "Slow pan right, revealing the landscape"
- "Static camera, clouds drift across the sky"

WRONG (will likely cause shake):
- "Camera walks through the forest"
- "POV of someone walking down the path"
- "Handheld shot following the subject"
- "Camera moves forward as the person walks"

- impossible movement

The video should feel like a natural continuation of the still image.

## Element Persistence Rule (CRITICAL)

Every element visible in Frame 1 MUST remain visible throughout the entire
video duration. This is the most important rule for video continuity.

### What this means:

1. **Before writing the video prompt, mentally list every element visible in Frame 1:**
   - People, animals, objects
   - Background elements (trees, buildings, clouds, water, sky)
   - Lighting elements (sun, moon, streetlights, reflections)
   - Atmospheric elements (fog, mist, haze, particles)

2. **Every element on that list MUST remain visible for the entire video.**
   The video prompt must not describe any element leaving, fading, vanishing,
   stopping, or disappearing.

3. **If an element is moving in Frame 1, it must continue moving — not stop.**
   Example: if birds are flying in Frame 1, the video prompt must describe them
   as continuously flying throughout the video. Never describe them as "flying
   across and then disappearing" or "flying into the distance and vanishing."

4. **Do NOT describe elements that were visible in Frame 1 as ceasing to exist
   or moving out of frame.** The video should feel like a continuous, unbroken
   observation of the same scene.

### Forbidden descriptions in video prompts:

- Elements leaving the frame (walking out, flying away, driving off)
- Elements fading, dimming, or vanishing
- Elements stopping their motion (wind stopping, water becoming still, clouds dispersing)
- Elements being obscured or covered up
- Scene transitions or cuts
- Time-lapse effects that would cause elements to disappear

The video prompt should describe a **continuous, unbroken shot** where every
element visible in Frame 1 remains present and active throughout the entire
duration.
