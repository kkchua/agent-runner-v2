# Thematic Variance Objective
CRITICAL: All variance MUST strictly follow the ORIGINAL SEMANTIC THEME of the source image description. Do not change the core subject identity or the fundamental setting.

Your objective is to generate creative variations that explore different photographic styles, compositions, and lighting, while keeping the exact same semantic subject and environment.

Example transformations (PRESERVING original semantic theme):
- Original: Red koi fish in a lotus pond → Red koi swimming under lily pads → Red koi resting near pond edge → Red koi gliding through deep water
- Original: Vintage car on a coastal road → Vintage car driving past cliffs → Vintage car parked by the ocean → Vintage car on a winding coastal highway
- Original: Cyberpunk street vendor → Vendor handing food to customer → Vendor cleaning the cart → Close up of vendor's hands working

FORBIDDEN transformations (Violates semantic theme):
- Red koi fish → salmon or jellyfish (Changed subject species)
- Coastal road → forest path (Changed fundamental setting)
- Street vendor → astronaut (Changed core identity)

Vary freely across:
- Photographic approach (drawn randomly from the style pool)
- Camera position, framing, and angles
- Lighting, mood, and atmosphere
- Subject pose, action, or minor interactions within the same scene

Keep the original semantic theme strictly consistent while exploring creative photographic variety.

# Thematic Variance Rules
The ORIGINAL SEMANTIC THEME MUST remain strictly consistent across every variant.

## Original Semantic Theme (STRICTLY PRESERVE)
- Core specific subject identity (e.g., if it's a "golden retriever", it MUST remain a "golden retriever", not change to a "poodle" or "wolf")
- Fundamental setting/location (e.g., if it's a "snowy mountain cabin", it must remain a "snowy mountain cabin", not change to a "beach hut")
- Core object relationships and scene context

## Allowed Variations (COMPOSITIONAL & PHOTOGRAPHIC)
- Subject pose, action, or interaction CAN vary within the scene
- Camera framing CAN vary (e.g., close-up → wide shot → medium shot)
- Camera angle CAN vary (e.g., eye-level → low angle → high angle)
- Photographic style CAN vary (drawn from the random pool)
- Lighting, weather, and time of day CAN vary (provided it logically fits the original setting)

## Forbidden Variations
- DO NOT change the species, object type, or core identity of the subject.
- DO NOT change the fundamental environment to a completely different biome or setting.
- DO NOT introduce entirely new primary subjects.

# Visual Style Rules
CRITICAL: ALL image prompts (t2i_prompt1) MUST produce photorealistic,
photographic-quality images. The generated images are used as Frame 1 for
video generation (image-to-video). Non-photographic styles (painting,
illustration, anime, cartoon) produce poor video results with flickering,
morphing, and temporal inconsistency.
## Mandatory Style: Photorealistic Photography
Every variant MUST use a photographic style. Visual variety between variants
comes from DIFFERENT PHOTOGRAPHIC TECHNIQUES, not different art mediums.
IMPORTANT: The restriction is on RENDERING STYLE only, not on SUBJECT MATTER.
Fantasy, sci-fi, surreal, dreamlike, and imaginary subjects are ALL ALLOWED —
they must simply be rendered as if photographed by a real camera. Think of it
as a movie still or a high-end VFX shot, not a painting.
Examples of allowed photorealistic non-real subjects:
- Sci-fi alien planet with twin suns (rendered like a movie VFX still)
- Fantasy forest with bioluminescent trees (rendered like a cinematic shot)
- Surreal dreamlike scene with floating islands (rendered like a photograph)
- Cyberpunk cityscape at night (rendered like editorial photography)
- Underwater magical realm (rendered like nature documentary footage)
Examples of forbidden renderings:
- The same sci-fi planet painted in oil painting style
- The same fantasy forest as a watercolor illustration
- The same dreamlike scene as anime or cartoon
## Allowed Photographic Styles
- Natural photography (outdoor, available light)
- Cinematic photography (dramatic lighting, film-like color grading)
- Editorial photography (magazine-quality, polished)
- Landscape photography (wide depth of field, sharp detail)
- Macro photography (extreme close-up, shallow depth of field)
- Wildlife photography (telephoto compression, natural behavior)
- Architectural photography (clean lines, perspective control)
- Street photography (candid, urban atmosphere)
- Fine art photography (creative lighting, mood-driven — but still photographic)
- Long exposure photography (motion blur on water, light trails)
## Strictly Forbidden Styles (Will Break Video Rendering)
- Oil painting / impasto / brushstroke textures
- Watercolor / ink wash
- Digital painting / concept art / matte painting
- Anime / cartoon / cel-shaded
- Illustration / pen and ink / graphic art
- Sketch / charcoal / pencil
- 3D render / CGI look
- Pixel art / voxel
- Pointillism / stipple
- Any non-photographic art medium
## How to Add "painterly" or "artistic" Quality
If the source image description suggests an artistic feel, achieve it through
PHOTOGRAPHIC means only:
- Soft bokeh and shallow depth of field (not brushstrokes)
- Golden hour or blue hour lighting (not painted skies)
- Film grain and analog color tones (not watercolor washes)
- Lens flare and optical effects (not digital painting overlays)
- Selective focus and tilt-shift (not illustration techniques)
## Variant Differentiation Through Photography (RANDOMIZED)
CRITICAL: Do NOT use a fixed variant-number → style mapping.
Do NOT default to the same style set every time.
Each variant must use a DIFFERENT photographic approach, drawn randomly
from the pool below. The selection must vary between images and between runs.

### Photographic Style Pool
- Natural photography (outdoor, available light, faithful to scene)
- Cinematic photography (dramatic side lighting, deep shadows, film grading)
- Golden hour photography (warm directional light, long shadows, amber tones)
- Blue hour / twilight photography (cool ambient light, soft glow)
- Moody / atmospheric photography (fog, mist, overcast, muted desaturated tones)
- High contrast photography (strong directional light, bold shadows, vivid colors)
- Low-key photography (dark scene, single-source chiaroscuro lighting)
- High-key photography (bright, airy, minimal shadows)
- Backlit / rim-light photography (sun behind subject, flare, silhouette edges)
- Long exposure photography (silky water, light trails, motion blur)
- Macro photography (extreme close-up, razor-thin depth of field)
- Telephoto compression (flattened depth, subject isolation)
- Wide-angle environmental photography (deep focus, expansive scene)
- Night photography (practical lights, neon, moonlight)
- Storm / dramatic weather photography (rain, wind, dark clouds)
- Overcast soft-light photography (diffused, shadowless, even)
- Analog film photography (grain, vintage color response)
- Editorial / magazine photography (polished, art-directed)
- Aerial / drone photography (top-down or high oblique)
- Street / candid photography (urban, spontaneous feel)

### Random Style Draw (MANDATORY before writing variants)
Before generating prompts for EACH image, run this shell command to randomly
draw N styles (replace N with the num_variants value):

shuf -e "natural" "cinematic" "golden-hour" "blue-hour" "moody" "high-contrast" "low-key" "high-key" "backlit" "long-exposure" "macro" "telephoto" "wide-angle" "night" "storm" "overcast" "analog-film" "editorial" "aerial" "street" | head -n N

Assign the drawn styles to Variant 1..N in the order returned.
If shell execution is not available, internally simulate a random shuffle
and ensure the selected set is NOT the fixed default set.
This guarantees a different style set for every image and every run.

### Random Selection Rules
1. Before writing prompts for each image, perform the Random Style Draw above
   to select N different styles for the N variants.
2. The drawn set MUST differ from run to run and from image to image.
3. FORBIDDEN DEFAULT: never fall back to the fixed set
   {natural, cinematic, golden hour, moody, high contrast} as an automatic choice.
4. Pair each drawn style with a UNIQUE combination of:
   environment + time of day + weather + camera position + subject variation.
5. No two variants in one set may share the same primary style,
   the same time of day, or the same weather condition.
6. If num_variants > 5 or you need further variety, also vary:
   - Different focal lengths (wide angle vs telephoto compression)
   - Different depth of field (deep focus vs creamy bokeh)
   - Different perspectives (low angle, high angle, eye level, Dutch angle)
   - Different weather conditions (clear, overcast, rain, snow — photographic)
   - Different times of day (dawn, midday, sunset, blue hour, night)
All variants MUST remain photorealistic. No exceptions.
