# Visual Style Rules

CRITICAL: ALL image prompts (t2i_prompt1) MUST produce photorealistic,
photographic-quality images. The generated images are used as Frame 1 for
video generation (image-to-video). Non-photographic styles (painting,
illustration, anime, cartoon) produce poor video results with flickering,
morphing, and temporal inconsistency.

## Mandatory Style: Photorealistic Photography

Every variant MUST use a photographic style. Visual variety between variants
comes from DIFFERENT PHOTOGRAPHIC TECHNIQUES, not different art mediums.

IMPORTANT: The restriction is on RENDERING STYLE only, not on SUBJECT MATTER.
Fantasy, sci-fi, surreal, dreamlike, and imaginary subjects are ALL ALLOWED —
they must simply be rendered as if photographed by a real camera. Think of it
as a movie still or a high-end VFX shot, not a painting.

Examples of allowed photorealistic non-real subjects:
- Sci-fi alien planet with twin suns (rendered like a movie VFX still)
- Fantasy forest with bioluminescent trees (rendered like a cinematic shot)
- Surreal dreamlike scene with floating islands (rendered like a photograph)
- Cyberpunk cityscape at night (rendered like editorial photography)
- Underwater magical realm (rendered like nature documentary footage)

Examples of forbidden renderings:
- The same sci-fi planet painted in oil painting style
- The same fantasy forest as a watercolor illustration
- The same dreamlike scene as anime or cartoon

## Allowed Photographic Styles

- Natural photography (outdoor, available light)
- Cinematic photography (dramatic lighting, film-like color grading)
- Editorial photography (magazine-quality, polished)
- Landscape photography (wide depth of field, sharp detail)
- Macro photography (extreme close-up, shallow depth of field)
- Wildlife photography (telephoto compression, natural behavior)
- Architectural photography (clean lines, perspective control)
- Street photography (candid, urban atmosphere)
- Fine art photography (creative lighting, mood-driven — but still photographic)
- Long exposure photography (motion blur on water, light trails)

## Strictly Forbidden Styles (Will Break Video Rendering)

- Oil painting / impasto / brushstroke textures
- Watercolor / ink wash
- Digital painting / concept art / matte painting
- Anime / cartoon / cel-shaded
- Illustration / pen and ink / graphic art
- Sketch / charcoal / pencil
- 3D render / CGI look
- Pixel art / voxel
- Pointillism / stipple
- Any non-photographic art medium

## How to Add "painterly" or "artistic" Quality

If the source image description suggests an artistic feel, achieve it through
PHOTOGRAPHIC means only:
- Soft bokeh and shallow depth of field (not brushstrokes)
- Golden hour or blue hour lighting (not painted skies)
- Film grain and analog color tones (not watercolor washes)
- Lens flare and optical effects (not digital painting overlays)
- Selective focus and tilt-shift (not illustration techniques)

## Variant Differentiation Through Photography

Each variant should use a DIFFERENT photographic approach:

- Variant 1: Natural photography — faithful to the source image lighting and mood
- Variant 2: Cinematic — dramatic side lighting, deeper shadows, film color grading
- Variant 3: Golden hour — warm directional light, long shadows, amber tones
- Variant 4: Moody/atmospheric — fog, mist, overcast, muted desaturated tones
- Variant 5: High contrast — strong directional light, bold shadows, vivid colors

If num_variants > 5, continue varying through:
- Different focal lengths (wide angle vs telephoto compression)
- Different depth of field (deep focus vs creamy bokeh)
- Different perspectives (low angle, high angle, eye level, Dutch angle)
- Different weather conditions (clear, overcast, rain, snow — photographic)
- Different times of day (dawn, midday, sunset, blue hour, night)

All variants MUST remain photorealistic. No exceptions.

# Video Prompt Rules

Assume the generated image is Frame 1. The video prompt (t2v_prompt1)
describes how the EXACT contents of Frame 1 move over the video duration.

## Camera Motion — STABLE ONLY (CRITICAL)

The video API sometimes misinterprets camera motion descriptions as handheld
walking, which produces an unpleasant footstep-bounce shake effect.

### ABSOLUTE RULE: Use smooth, stable camera motion only

Camera movement must feel like it was shot on a professional dolly, crane,
or gimbal — NEVER like a person walking holding a camera.

### Safe camera motions (use these):

- **Static camera** — no camera movement, only subject/environment motion
- **Slow dolly in/out** — smooth forward/backward glide (NOT walking)
- **Slow pan** — smooth horizontal rotation
- **Slow tilt** — smooth vertical rotation
- **Slow push-in** — gradual zoom-like approach (not walking forward)
- **Slow pull-back** — gradual retreat (not walking backward)

### Forbidden camera descriptions (will cause shake):

- Walking / walking toward / walking through
- Handheld / shaky / documentary style
- POV / point-of-view / what you see
- Following a subject (implies walking behind them)
- "Camera moves through" (implies walking path)
- Any description that suggests body movement or footstep rhythm
- Fast or jerky movements
- Random camera adjustments

### When in doubt, use "static camera"

A locked-off camera with only subject/environment motion is ALWAYS
preferred over any camera movement that could be misinterpreted as walking.

If you are unsure whether a camera motion will be stable, use "static camera"
or omit camera motion entirely — let the scene move naturally.

### Camera motion examples

CORRECT:
- "Static camera, gentle waves lap at the shore"
- "Slow dolly forward, the subject walks toward the camera"
- "Slow pan right, revealing the landscape"
- "Static camera, clouds drift across the sky"

WRONG (will likely cause shake):
- "Camera walks through the forest"
- "POV of someone walking down the path"
- "Handheld shot following the subject"
- "Camera moves forward as the person walks"

## THE GOLDEN RULE

Before writing ANY word in the video prompt, mentally list every object,
surface, and element visible in Frame 1. The video prompt may ONLY
reference elements from that list. If an element is not in Frame 1, it
does not exist in the video — period.

## Video Prompt Template

Structure every t2v_prompt1 using this exact pattern:

"[Camera motion], the [existing element 1] [motion verb], the [existing
element 2] [motion verb], [atmospheric detail about existing elements]."

Every noun in the prompt MUST be an element visible in Frame 1.
Every verb MUST come from the Allowed Verbs list below.

## Allowed Verbs (motion of existing elements only)

Camera verbs: dolly, pan, tilt, crane, orbit, push-in, pull-back, track
Water verbs: flow, ripple, lap, crash, surge, drift, reflect, shimmer
Vegetation verbs: sway, bend, rustle (existing trees/plants only — NOT growing)
Cloud/sky verbs: drift, shift, roll (existing clouds only — NOT forming)
Light verbs: shift, fade, shimmer, dance (existing light only)
Subject verbs: walk, swim, float, glide, turn, move (existing subjects only)
Particle verbs: drift, float, scatter (existing particles only)

## Forbidden Verbs (imply creation, appearance, or disappearance)

NEVER use these verbs — they cause objects to appear/disappear in video:

- grow, growing, sprout, emerge, materialize
- appear, appearing, fade in, come into view
- disappear, vanish, fade out, dissolve
- transform, morph, become, turn into
- generate, create, form, develop
- rise (when implying new objects rising into frame)
- fall (when implying new objects falling into frame — falling leaves, rain)
- enter, arrive, approach (new subjects from off-screen)
- leave, depart, exit (existing subjects leaving frame)

## Forbidden Elements (regardless of scene context)

These elements MUST NOT appear in the video prompt unless ALREADY VISIBLE
in Frame 1. The video API will render whatever the prompt describes — if
you mention it, it appears, even if it wasn't in the source image.

- Birds, bats, insects, butterflies (any flying creatures)
- Falling leaves, petals, seeds, debris
- Fish jumping, dolphins breaching
- Rain, snow (unless visible in Frame 1)
- Boats, vehicles, people not in Frame 1
- Trees, rocks, structures not in Frame 1
- Fog, mist, haze (unless visible in Frame 1)

## Scene-Specific Hallucination Triggers (HIGH RISK)

The video API has strong trained associations that override negative prompts.
For these scenes, you MUST include positive counter-instructions in t2v_prompt1.

Beach/Ocean/Sea:
  TRIGGER: API associates water scenes with birds, seagulls, dolphins
  MUST INCLUDE in t2v_prompt1: "clear empty sky above the water"
  DO describe: wave motion, foam, surface reflections, sand texture
  DO NOT describe: palm trees, seagulls, dolphins, boats, shells

Forest/Woodland:
  TRIGGER: API associates forests with falling leaves, deer, birds
  MUST INCLUDE in t2v_prompt1: "still air, no falling leaves"
  DO describe: tree sway (existing trees), light filtering through canopy
  DO NOT describe: falling leaves, deer, birds, mushrooms

Mountain/Cliff:
  TRIGGER: API associates mountains with eagles, clouds forming, snow
  MUST INCLUDE in t2v_prompt1: "empty sky, no birds soaring"
  DO describe: existing cloud drift, light shifting on peaks
  DO NOT describe: eagles, clouds forming, snow falling

Waterfall/River:
  TRIGGER: API associates waterfalls with mist rising, fish, rainbow
  MUST INCLUDE in t2v_prompt1: "no mist rising above the falls"
  DO describe: water flow, existing mist movement, spray
  DO NOT describe: rainbow, fish, rocks appearing

City/Urban:
  TRIGGER: API associates cities with cars appearing, people walking in
  MUST INCLUDE in t2v_prompt1: "static scene, no new vehicles or people entering"
  DO describe: existing traffic flow, existing lights, existing movement
  DO NOT describe: cars appearing, people walking in, lights turning on

Desert/Arid:
  TRIGGER: API associates deserts with sand dunes shifting, camels
  MUST INCLUDE in t2v_prompt1: "still sand surface, no wind-blown sand"
  DO describe: heat shimmer, existing sand texture, stillness
  DO NOT describe: cacti appearing, sand dunes shifting dramatically

IMPORTANT: The "MUST INCLUDE" phrases are POSITIVE instructions that tell
the API what the scene SHOULD look like. These are far more effective than
negative prompts alone. Always include them for these scene types.

## Correct vs Incorrect Examples

WRONG: "Gentle waves lap at the shore as palm trees sway and seagulls
soar overhead"
→ WRONG because: palm trees and seagulls are not in Frame 1

CORRECT: "Slow dolly forward, gentle waves lap at the sandy shore,
soft foam drifts along the waterline, subtle light shimmer on the
water surface"
→ CORRECT because: every element (waves, sand, foam, light) is in Frame 1

WRONG: "The forest comes alive as leaves gently fall and a deer steps
through the trees"
→ WRONG because: falling leaves and deer are not in Frame 1

CORRECT: "Static camera, existing tree canopy sways gently in the wind,
dappled light shifts across the forest floor, subtle atmospheric haze
drifts between the trees"
→ CORRECT because: trees, light, and haze are all in Frame 1

## Double Object Prevention

The video API sometimes duplicates prominent objects (moon, sun, planets)
creating two or more copies in the sky. To prevent this:

If Frame 1 contains a moon, sun, or other celestial object:
- Mention it EXACTLY ONCE in the video prompt
- Use "the single moon" or "the one sun" — the word "single" prevents duplication
- Do NOT describe it moving or changing — just say "the single moon remains
  stationary in the sky" or "the single sun casts steady light"
- Do NOT describe multiple light sources

If Frame 1 contains any prominent singular object (lighthouse, tower, statue):
- Mention it EXACTLY ONCE
- Use "the single [object]" to prevent duplication
- Do NOT describe it being replicated or multiplied
