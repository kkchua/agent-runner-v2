Your mission is to convert every individual image description JSON generated in STEP 01 into a high-quality Image Prompt JSON and Video Prompt.

This step is a reconstruction task, NOT a summarization task.

The objective is to regenerate an image that is visually indistinguishable from the original source image while producing multiple high-quality photographic interpretations.

# Thematic Variance Rules

The CONCEPT category MUST remain consistent across every variant.

## Concept Category (KEEP)
- Core subject type (e.g., aquatic life, landscape, wildlife, architecture)
- Overall thematic family (e.g., nature, urban, people, objects)
- General mood direction (e.g., serene, dynamic, mysterious)

## Subject Variation (ALLOW)
- Specific subject identity CAN vary within category
- Number of subjects CAN vary
- Subject species/type CAN vary (e.g., different fish breeds, different tree types)
- Subject presence CAN vary (some variants may omit secondary subjects)

## Scene Variation (ALLOW)
- Environment CAN vary (e.g., pond → river → ocean → aquarium)
- Location CAN vary (e.g., park → forest → garden → urban)
- Weather CAN vary (e.g., sunny → rainy → foggy → snowy)
- Time of day CAN vary (e.g., dawn → midday → sunset → night)
- Season CAN vary (e.g., spring → summer → autumn → winter)

## Composition Variation (ALLOW)
- Framing CAN vary (e.g., close-up → wide shot → aerial)
- Camera angle CAN vary (e.g., eye-level → low angle → overhead)
- Depth layers CAN vary based on scene needs

# Visual Style Rules

CRITICAL: ALL image prompts (t2i_prompt1) MUST produce photorealistic,
photographic-quality images. The generated images are used as Frame 1 for
video generation (image-to-video). Non-photographic styles (painting,
illustration, anime, cartoon) produce poor video results with flickering,
morphing, and temporal inconsistency.

## Mandatory Style: Photorealistic Photography

Every variant MUST use a photographic style. Visual variety between variants
comes from DIFFERENT PHOTOGRAPHIC TECHNIQUES, not different art mediums.

IMPORTANT: The restriction is on RENDERING STYLE only, not on SUBJECT MATTER.
Fantasy, sci-fi, surreal, dreamlike, and imaginary subjects are ALL ALLOWED —
they must simply be rendered as if photographed by a real camera. Think of it
as a movie still or a high-end VFX shot, not a painting.

Examples of allowed photorealistic non-real subjects:
- Sci-fi alien planet with twin suns (rendered like a movie VFX still)
- Fantasy forest with bioluminescent trees (rendered like a cinematic shot)
- Surreal dreamlike scene with floating islands (rendered like a photograph)
- Cyberpunk cityscape at night (rendered like editorial photography)
- Underwater magical realm (rendered like nature documentary footage)

Examples of forbidden renderings:
- The same sci-fi planet painted in oil painting style
- The same fantasy forest as a watercolor illustration
- The same dreamlike scene as anime or cartoon

## Allowed Photographic Styles

- Natural photography (outdoor, available light)
- Cinematic photography (dramatic lighting, film-like color grading)
- Editorial photography (magazine-quality, polished)
- Landscape photography (wide depth of field, sharp detail)
- Macro photography (extreme close-up, shallow depth of field)
- Wildlife photography (telephoto compression, natural behavior)
- Architectural photography (clean lines, perspective control)
- Street photography (candid, urban atmosphere)
- Fine art photography (creative lighting, mood-driven — but still photographic)
- Long exposure photography (motion blur on water, light trails)

## Strictly Forbidden Styles (Will Break Video Rendering)

- Oil painting / impasto / brushstroke textures
- Watercolor / ink wash
- Digital painting / concept art / matte painting
- Anime / cartoon / cel-shaded
- Illustration / pen and ink / graphic art
- Sketch / charcoal / pencil
- 3D render / CGI look
- Pixel art / voxel
- Pointillism / stipple
- Any non-photographic art medium

## How to Add "painterly" or "artistic" Quality

If the source image description suggests an artistic feel, achieve it through
PHOTOGRAPHIC means only:

- Soft bokeh and shallow depth of field (not brushstrokes)
- Golden hour or blue hour lighting (not painted skies)
- Film grain and analog color tones (not watercolor washes)
- Lens flare and optical effects (not digital painting overlays)
- Selective focus and tilt-shift (not illustration techniques)

## Variant Differentiation Through Photography (Randomized Selection)

CRITICAL: Do NOT use a fixed variant-number → style mapping.
Do NOT default to the same style set every time.

### Photographic Style Pool

Treat the following as a pool. Each variant draws a DIFFERENT entry.
Selection must vary between runs and between images.

- Natural available-light photography
- Cinematic photography (dramatic lighting, film-like color grading)
- Golden hour photography (warm directional light, long shadows, amber tones)
- Blue hour / twilight photography (cool ambient light)
- Moody atmospheric photography (fog, mist, overcast, muted desaturated tones)
- High contrast photography (hard directional light, bold shadows, vivid colors)
- Low-key photography (dark scene, single-source chiaroscuro lighting)
- High-key photography (bright, airy, minimal shadows)
- Backlit / rim-light photography (sun behind subject, flare)
- Long exposure photography (silky water, light trails)
- Macro photography (extreme close-up, razor-thin focus)
- Telephoto compression photography (flattened depth, subject isolation)
- Wide-angle environmental photography (deep focus, expansive scene)
- Night photography (practical lights, neon, moonlight)
- Storm / dramatic weather photography (rain, wind, dark clouds)
- Overcast soft-light photography (diffused, shadowless)
- Analog film photography (grain, vintage color response)
- Editorial / magazine photography (polished, art-directed)
- Aerial / drone photography (top-down or high oblique)

### Random Selection Rules

1. Before writing prompts, internally shuffle the pool and draw N different styles for the N variants.
2. The drawn set MUST differ from run to run and from image to image. Never reuse a fixed sequence.
3. FORBIDDEN DEFAULT: never fall back to the fixed set {natural, cinematic, golden hour, moody, high contrast}. Selection must not be predictable.
4. Pair each drawn style with a UNIQUE combination of: environment + time of day + weather + camera position + subject variation.
5. No two variants in one set may share the same primary style, the same time of day, or the same weather.
6. For additional differentiation, freely combine each drawn style with:
   - Different focal lengths (wide angle vs telephoto compression)
   - Different depth of field (deep focus vs creamy bokeh)
   - Different perspectives (low angle, high angle, eye level, Dutch angle)
7. All variants MUST remain photorealistic. No exceptions.
